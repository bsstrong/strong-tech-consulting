# Mississippi closure source extracts

## Specific authorization rule

[Mississippi Code section 21-17-5(2), current 2025 text](https://law.justia.com/codes/mississippi/title-21/chapter-17/section-21-17-5/) says that unless another Mississippi statute or law specifically authorizes the action, municipal home rule does not authorize municipal governing authorities to “levy taxes of any kind or increase the levy of any authorized tax.”

The [Mississippi Secretary of State's official-code gateway](https://www.sos.ms.gov/publications-external-affairs/mississippi-law) identifies LexisNexis as the official publisher of the searchable unannotated Mississippi Code.

## State Auditor municipal tax framework

The official 2022 Municipal Audit and Accounting Guide states that municipal budget revenues must be defined by type of tax, fee, grant, or loan. Its chart of accounts classifies tax receipts as general property taxes, penalties/interest, and `Other Taxes`, and separately identifies privilege licenses and state-shared general sales tax.

The municipal compliance questionnaire's “Taxes and Other Receipts” section tests ad valorem taxes, local privilege taxes under section 27-17-5, and transient-vendor taxes. The guide's suggested “Other Taxes” audit procedure identifies local privilege, vending-machine, and transient-vendor taxes. Searches of the complete extracted guide found no `local income tax`, `municipal income tax`, `tax on wages`, `earnings tax`, or `payroll tax`.

## Legislative proposals

The official 1997, 1998, 1999, 2001, and 2003 “All Measures” reports each identify one bill titled “Local income tax; authorize certain municipalities/cities to levy.” The corresponding official histories show that every measure died in House committee.

The introduced texts would have authorized eligible cities to impose 1% to 1.5% of state taxable income on persons earning or receiving income from employment or business carried out in the city. They included work allocation for nonresidents and state tax-administration provisions. These texts are proposed bills, not enacted authority.

## Job Development Assessment Fee exclusion

Mississippi Department of Revenue individual-income-tax instructions describe an assessment on employees whose wages help pay Mississippi Business Finance Corporation bonds that created their jobs. DOR tells taxpayers that the amount may appear in the W-2 city/local withholding box with `RED` as the local name. That reporting convention does not make the corporation-bond assessment a municipality-imposed tax.
