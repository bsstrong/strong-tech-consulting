# West-state municipal employee-wage withholding audit

Audit date: 2026-08-25. Sources are current primary government sources unless an older official source date is stated. This file is research evidence only; it does not modify the registry.

## Inclusion test

A jurisdiction qualifies only when a current municipal tax is directly imposed as a percentage of employee wages or compensation, reaches a nonresident because the employee works at or reports to a worksite in the municipality, and requires employer withholding/remittance. Employer-only taxes, resident-only taxes, flat/head/occupational-privilege fees, counties, regions, schools, special districts, business taxes, and self-employment taxes are excluded.

`NO_AUTHORITY_CONFIRMED` is used only where the primary-source set closes the relevant municipal universe. Where a statute contains an unclosed exception, home-rule authority remains unsearched municipality by municipality, or no official statewide adopter list exists, the result is `UNDETERMINED` even if no positive was found.

## State evidence notes

### California — UNDETERMINED

- **Verified:** California Government Code section 50026 says that a local agency otherwise authorized by law or charter to impose a tax on an employee's privilege of working, measured by earnings or earned income, may not apply it to nonresident employees unless the same tax, rate, and credits apply to residents employed there. The section expressly says it does not authorize a tax otherwise prohibited. [California Government Code, title 5, division 1, part 1, chapter 1, article 2](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?article=2.&chapter=1.&division=1.&lawCode=GOV&part=1.&title=5.) (current code accessed 2026-08-25).
- **Verified exclusion:** San Francisco's current Administrative Office Tax is 1.47% of payroll expense of an administrative-office business for tax year 2025. It is imposed on the business, not withheld from employee wages. [San Francisco Treasurer & Tax Collector — Administrative Office Tax](https://www.sftreasurer.org/business/taxes-fees/administrative-office-tax-aot-0).
- **Inference/gap:** Section 50026 confirms that charter or statutory authority is legally possible, not that any current adopter exists. A complete official charter/adopter and employer-withholding universe was not located; zero candidates is not a closed negative.

### Colorado — UNDETERMINED

- **Verified statutory-city rule:** Section 31-15-501(1)(c) authorizes a municipality to license, regulate, and tax occupations and businesses. Its official annotations distinguish a fixed occupation tax from an income tax and state that a tax on income exceeds powers delegated to Colorado municipalities. [2026 Colorado Revised Statutes, title 31, section 31-15-501 and annotations](https://olls.info/crs/crs2026-title-31.pdf) (official 2026 edition; section begins at PDF page 378 and annotations at page 383). [2026 CRS titles index](https://content.leg.colorado.gov/agencies/office-legislative-legal-services/2026-crs-titles-download).
- **Verified excluded flat taxes:** Glendale withholds $5 per month from an employee working in the city who earns more than $750 in the month and imposes a separate $5 employer portion. [Glendale Occupational Privilege Tax](https://www.glendale.co.us/355/Occupational-Privilege-Tax). Greenwood Village imposes $2 per month on the employee and $2 on the employer when the employee earns at least $250 in the month. [Greenwood Village Occupational Privilege Tax](https://greenwoodvillage.com/1220/Occupational-Privilege-Tax-OPT). Sheridan imposes a $3 monthly employee payroll deduction and separate $3 employer tax for work in Sheridan, explicitly without regard to residence. [Sheridan Occupational Privilege Tax](https://www.ci.sheridan.co.us/288/Occupational-Privilege-Tax). All are flat amounts, not wage percentages, and are excluded.
- **Verified excluded/repealed example:** Aurora repealed its occupational privilege tax effective 2025-01-01. [Aurora Occupational Privilege Tax](https://www.auroragov.org/business_services/taxes/occupational_privilege_tax).
- **Inference/gap:** The official annotations strongly address statutory municipalities, while Colorado home-rule cities have separate constitutional authority. No exhaustive official list of all home-rule payroll/occupation taxes was found, so no statewide negative is claimed.

### Hawaii — NO_AUTHORITY_CONFIRMED

- **Verified closed universe:** The State Department of Budget and Finance says Hawaii has the City and County of Honolulu, Counties of Maui, Hawaii, and Kauai, and quasi-county Kalawao, and expressly states: there are no independent or separate cities or other municipalities. [State of Hawaii Budget and Finance — About State Government / Counties and Their Relationship to the State](https://budget.hawaii.gov/budget/state-of-hawaii-background-information/) (current page accessed 2026-08-25).
- **Verified authority structure:** Article VIII, section 1 provides that the Legislature creates counties and may create other political subdivisions, each exercising powers conferred under general laws. [Hawaii Constitution, article VIII, section 1](https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol01_Ch0001-0042F/05-CONST/CONST_0008-0001.htm). The State Department of Taxation identifies four counties and says each administers real-property and motor-vehicle taxes. [Hawaii Department of Taxation — Useful Links / Counties](https://tax.hawaii.gov/useful/).
- **Disposition:** Because the audit excludes counties/regions and the State affirmatively closes the independent municipal universe at zero, no qualifying municipal wage tax can exist under the defined unit of analysis. This conclusion does not classify county taxes.

### Idaho — UNDETERMINED

- **Verified authority rule:** The Idaho Supreme Court held that municipal taxes require specific legislative authorization and that Idaho Constitution article VII, section 6 is not self-executing; Idaho Code section 50-301 is not a general taxing grant. [Idaho Supreme Court opinion, City of Pocatello v. State (2014)](https://isc.idaho.gov/opinions/41316X.pdf).
- **Verified inspected grant:** Idaho Code section 50-1044 permits resort cities to seek voter authorization for local-option nonproperty taxes "as provided herein." Section 50-1046 enumerates lodging occupancy, liquor/wine/beer by the drink, and sales taxes on transactions subject to state sales tax; it does not include employee wages. [Idaho Code §50-1044](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title50/T50CH10/SECT50-1044/); [§50-1046](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title50/T50CH10/SECT50-1046/); [§50-1047](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title50/T50CH10/SECT50-1047/); [§50-1049](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title50/T50CH10/SECT50-1049/) (current code pages accessed 2026-08-25).
- **Verified current administrator category:** The State Tax Commission's current city-tax page describes and lists resort-city local sales taxes and links Title 50, chapter 10 as the governing law. [Idaho State Tax Commission — City Sales Taxes](https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/sales-use/sales-tax/local-sales-tax/city-sales-tax/) (current 2026 page).
- **Inference/gap:** The specific-authorization rule is clear, but the inspected Title 50 grant and Tax Commission sales-tax list do not affirmatively certify that no special wage-tax grant exists anywhere else in Idaho law. The result remains `UNDETERMINED` rather than over-reading a negative search.

### Montana — UNDETERMINED

- **Verified:** Current MCA 7-1-112 prohibits a local government with self-government powers from authorizing a tax on income unless the power is specifically delegated by law. [Montana Code Annotated 7-1-112](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0070/chapter_0010/part_0010/section_0120/0070-0010-0010-0120.html) (2025 MCA/current page accessed 2026-08-25).
- **Verified context:** Montana's current individual income tax is the state program codified in Title 15, chapter 30. [MCA Title 15, chapter 30 index](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0150/chapter_0300/parts_index.html).
- **Inference/gap:** No wage/payroll/income delegation to a city or town was found in the current MCA searches, but MCA 7-1-112 contains an express delegation exception and no official exhaustive delegation/adopter list was located. The source set therefore cannot be labeled complete.

### New Mexico — NO_AUTHORITY_CONFIRMED

- **Verified prohibition/home-rule limit:** An official City of Santa Fe resolution quotes NMSA 1978 section 3-15-7 that a home-rule charter may not authorize a tax not specifically authorized by state law and section 3-18-2's prohibition on municipal income tax. [City of Santa Fe Resolution requesting clarification of taxing power](https://santafenm.gov/archive_center/document/10515) (official municipal record, 2014). A later official City Attorney analysis continues to apply section 3-18-2 as the governing boundary for municipal excise-tax authority. [City of Santa Fe City Attorney legal analysis](https://santafenm.gov/Legal_opinions_on_Ballot_Questions_2023_ekm.pdf) (2023).
- **Verified current statewide administrator universe:** New Mexico Taxation and Revenue states that municipal and county "local option taxes are gross receipts taxes," collected by the State, and directs every city to FYI-M121 for municipal options. [New Mexico Taxation and Revenue — Local Option Taxes](https://www.tax.newmexico.gov/governments/municipal-county-governments/local-option-taxes/) (current page accessed 2026-08-25); [FYI-M121, Municipal Gross Receipts Tax Local Options](https://www.tax.newmexico.gov/businesses/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/2020/11/FYI-M121-Municipal-Gross-Receipts-Tax-Programs-Final-Draft.pdf) (current publication surfaced by TRD in 2026).
- **Disposition:** The municipal income-tax prohibition covers home-rule municipalities, and the current state-administered municipal local-option universe is gross-receipts based rather than employee-wage based. No qualifying authority or adopter remains in the official source set.

### Oregon — PARTIAL (Eugene included)

- **Verified Eugene positive:** The Eugene sources in the candidate row directly establish employee-wage incidence, nonresident/work/reporting-location scope, employer withholding/remittance, current forms, and the 0.44% current rate tier.
- **Verified statewide legal possibility:** ORS 305.625 applies when a city or county ordinance collects an income tax by requiring employers to withhold from compensation of individuals employed within its boundaries; ORS 305.630 requires participating public employers to comply for employees whose regular place of employment is within the jurisdiction; ORS 305.635 addresses the local withholding rate and forms. [Oregon Revised Statutes 305.620-305.645](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors305.html) (2025 ORS page; the Legislature warns that 2025 special-session/2026-session changes require checking 2026 Oregon Laws).
- **Verified excluded Portland-administered programs:** Portland administers payroll withholding for Metro Supportive Housing Services and Multnomah County Preschool for All. Those are a regional and a county tax, not City of Portland taxes, and are excluded. [Portland Revenue — Personal Income Tax Withholding Information for Employers/Payroll Providers](https://www.portland.gov/revenue/withholding) (current page accessed 2026-08-25).
- **Other-city closure attempt and gap:** Targeted searches of current Oregon Legislature, Department of Revenue, and official city tax/payroll pages produced Eugene as the only directly supported city employee-wage tax. ORS nevertheless contemplates self-administered city ordinances and neither ORS nor DOR publishes an exhaustive adopter/agreement list. Portland's business income taxes are business/self-employment incidences, while the Arts Tax is resident-only and flat; neither qualifies. The statewide result is `PARTIAL`, not complete.

### South Dakota — UNDETERMINED

- **Verified:** SDCL 9-12-2 authorizes a municipality to levy and collect taxes on real property within statutory limits. [South Dakota Codified Laws, chapter 9-12](https://sdlegislature.gov/api/Statutes/9-12.html?all=true) (current through 2026 session material on the official service).
- **Verified current administrator program:** DOR states that South Dakota law allows municipalities to impose sales/use and gross-receipts taxes and publishes the complete municipal schedules for July-December 2026. [South Dakota Department of Revenue — Municipal Tax](https://dor.sd.gov/businesses/taxes/municipal-tax/).
- **Verified limitation with unresolved exception:** SDCL 6-12-14 restricts county/city/home-rule taxes similar to specified state revenue or regulatory taxes unless specifically authorized and preserves certain taxes imposed before 1996-03-01. [South Dakota Codified Laws, chapter 6-12](https://sdlegislature.gov/api/Statutes/6-12.html?all=true).
- **Inference/gap:** Because South Dakota has no state individual income-tax analog and the statute contains both specific-authorization and grandfather pathways, the DOR sales/gross-receipts schedule does not conclusively close every possible municipal wage ordinance.

### Texas — UNDETERMINED

- **Verified current statewide overview:** The Comptroller states that Texas has more than 1,200 incorporated cities and that cities can levy local property and sales taxes; it provides city sales-tax lookup/history tools. [Texas Comptroller — Texas Cities](https://comptroller.texas.gov/transparency/local/cities.php) (current page accessed 2026-08-25).
- **Verified administered local program:** The Comptroller's current statewide program describes voter-approved local sales-tax options and lists adopting cities. [Texas Comptroller — Sales Tax Programs](https://comptroller.texas.gov/economy/development/sales-tax/) (January 2026 resources); [Cities with Additional Sales and Use Tax](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/sales/city-additional-tax.php).
- **Inference/gap:** These sources positively identify property/sales authority but do not say that those are the only taxes a home-rule city may impose. No primary statewide wage-tax prohibition or exhaustive home-rule tax/adopter list was found, so zero candidates remains unclosed.

### Utah — UNDETERMINED

- **Verified constitutional boundary:** Utah Constitution article XI, section 5 grants charter cities tax power only "within the limits prescribed by general law." [Utah Constitution, article XI, section 5](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/ArticleXI/UC_AXI_S5_1800010118000101.pdf). Article XIII, section 5 says the Legislature may authorize political subdivisions by statute to assess and collect taxes for their purposes and restricts income-tax revenue uses. [Utah Constitution, article XIII](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Articlexiii/UC_AXIII_1800010118000101.pdf) (current constitution pages accessed 2026-08-25).
- **Verified inspected current municipal grants:** Current Title 10 contains specific municipal energy sales/use and telecommunications license taxes and specific municipal power provisions. [Utah Code Title 10, current consolidated PDF](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/title10/C10_1800010118000101.pdf); [Utah Code 10-1-403, municipal telecommunications license tax](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title10/Chapter1/10-1-S403.html); [Utah Code 10-8-86, transit sales/use tax](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title10/Chapter8/10-8-S86.html). The State Tax Commission's current rate page covers state/local sales and transaction taxes. [Utah State Tax Commission — Sales & Use Tax Rates](https://tax.utah.gov/business/sales-tax/sales/rates/).
- **Inference/gap:** No current general-law authorization for a municipal employee-wage tax was found, but neither the Constitution, Title 10 index, nor Tax Commission page expressly declares the inspected grants exhaustive. A definitive negative would require an authoritative complete delegation inventory.

### Wyoming — UNDETERMINED

- **Verified constitutional boundary:** Wyoming Constitution article 15, section 13 says no tax may be levied except pursuant to law. Section 18 contemplates a possible income tax but requires credits for sales/use and ad valorem taxes paid to Wyoming taxing authorities. [Wyoming Constitution, article 15](https://wyoleg.gov/NXT/gateway.dll/2025%20Wyoming%20Statutes/2025%20Constitution/1/16) (official 2025/current text accessed 2026-08-25).
- **Verified current statewide overview:** The Legislative Service Office states that local governments have sales, use, and property-tax imposition authority and receive other state-distributed revenues. [Wyoming Legislature — State finance visualizations](https://www.wyoleg.gov/stateFinances/visualizations) (current page accessed 2026-08-25). Current Title 39 contains the revenue-and-taxation statutes, including municipal sales/use provisions. [Wyoming Statutes Title 39](https://wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title39.pdf); [official current statutes landing page](https://www.wyoleg.gov/stateStatutes/StateStatutes).
- **Inference/gap:** The finance overview is not worded as an exclusive catalog, and article 15 does not itself bar a legislatively authorized local income tax. Without an official exhaustive delegation/adopter source, the result remains `UNDETERMINED`.
