# Idaho municipal worksite wage-tax closure

## Outcome

**Disposition: `NO_AUTHORITY_CONFIRMED`; zero registry rows.**

As of August 26, 2026, no Idaho municipality has legal authority to impose the percentage-based, employer-withheld employee tax covered by this registry. Idaho therefore moves from `UNDETERMINED` to a closed zero. The national registry remains 2,815 municipality rows across nine states.

This iteration took approximately 22 minutes, within the one-hour state cap.

## Definition applied

The registry includes only a municipality or city-like government's percentage tax calculated from an employee's wages, earnings, compensation, or payroll because the employee works there, including a nonresident commuter, with employer-withholding relevance.

It excludes resident-only income taxes, employer-only payroll-expense taxes, flat occupational or head taxes, sales/property/business taxes, county or district taxes, and taxes limited to business profits or self-employment.

## Why Idaho is closed

1. **A city needs a specific legislative tax grant.** [Idaho Constitution article VII, section 6](https://sos.idaho.gov/elections/publications/Idaho_Constitution.pdf) makes municipal taxing power dependent on legislative investment. In *North Idaho Building Contractors Association v. City of Hayden*, the Idaho Supreme Court held that the local-self-government language in Idaho Code section 50-301 is not a general taxing grant and that a municipal tax on the public requires specific legislative authorization. The case is docket 41316-2013; the [full opinion text](https://law.justia.com/cases/idaho/supreme-court-civil/2015/41316.html) was cross-checked because the Idaho Courts legacy PDF link returned 404 after its site migration.

2. **The special-law escape route is constitutionally closed.** [Article III, section 19](https://sos.idaho.gov/elections/publications/Idaho_Constitution.pdf) prohibits local or special laws for tax assessment and collection. A hidden municipality-specific special act therefore cannot supply the missing authority while that provision controls.

3. **The complete current code contains no qualifying general grant.** I retrieved all 74 official title indexes and all 1,471 linked chapter PDFs from the [Idaho Legislature's current code](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/). There were 1,470 valid PDFs. The one dead PDF link, Title 44 chapter 28, was recovered through its live chapter page and all four live sections. Searches across 37.2 MB of normalized statutory text found no municipal/city/local income, wage, or earnings tax authorization and no municipal percentage tax measured by employee compensation or payroll. All combined municipal/income/compensation candidate contexts were reviewed and were unrelated.

4. **The actual local-option grant excludes wages.** [Idaho Code section 50-1044](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title50/T50CH10/SECT50-1044/) authorizes resort-city local-option nonproperty taxes only as provided in that act. [Section 50-1046](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title50/T50CH10/SECT50-1046/) enumerates short-term lodging occupancy, liquor/wine/beer by the drink, and sales taxes on state-taxable sales. It does not include wages, compensation, earnings, or payroll. The current [Idaho State Tax Commission city-tax page](https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/sales-use/sales-tax/local-sales-tax/city-sales-tax/) likewise describes resort-city sales taxes and links that chapter.

The Tax Commission page is corroboration, not the negative-proof foundation. The foundation is the specific-authorization rule, the constitutional bar on local/special tax laws, and the complete current-code audit.

## Caveat and refresh rule

`NO_AUTHORITY_CONFIRMED` is a current legal conclusion, not a promise that Idaho law cannot change. Refresh this state after any amendment to Idaho Constitution article III or VII, Idaho Code section 50-301, Title 50 chapter 10, a new general municipal tax delegation, or controlling Idaho appellate law.
