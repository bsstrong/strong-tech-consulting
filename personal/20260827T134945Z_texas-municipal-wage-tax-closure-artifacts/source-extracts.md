# Texas source extracts and audit notes

Research date: August 27, 2026

## Controlling rule

Texas Constitution article VIII, section 1(f) provides a numerical ceiling: a county, city, or town occupation tax on a profession or business may not exceed one-half of the state occupation tax on the same pursuit.

In *Brown v. City of Galveston*, 75 S.W. 488 (Tex. 1903), the Texas Supreme Court applied that ceiling where the state imposed no occupation tax. The city's proposed occupation tax was invalid because one-half of zero is zero.

Attorney General Opinion JM-1195 (1990) states the same rule directly and applies it to air-conditioning and refrigeration contractors: the constitutional provision prohibits a municipality from imposing an occupation tax on a pursuit for which the state levies none.

In *Conlen Grain & Milling Co. v. Texas Grain Sorghum Producers Board*, 519 S.W.2d 620 (Tex. 1975), the Texas Supreme Court defines an occupation tax as an excise on a person for the privilege of carrying on a business, trade, or occupation. The court focuses on the taxed subject and holds that a quantity-based calculation did not change the classification.

Applied to the registry definition, a city percentage tax owed by an employee because the employee performs services in the city taxes the employee's occupation. Its wage measure does not evade the article VIII ceiling.

## Municipal authority

Texas Constitution article XI, section 5 permits home-rule cities to levy taxes authorized by law or charter, but charters and ordinances must remain consistent with the Constitution and general laws.

Texas Tax Code section 302.101 permits most municipalities to impose occupation taxes. Section 302.102 authorizes collection of taxes recognized by law or a home-rule charter. Neither provision supersedes article VIII, section 1(f).

Attorney General Letter Opinion 97-005 describes municipalities as strictly limited to taxing powers granted by the Constitution or statute and rejects using an agreement to impose a tax that law otherwise prohibits.

## Current-code cross-check

The current Texas Tax Code PDF was retrieved from the Legislature during this iteration. It was 250,874 bytes with SHA-256 `DBFBA8A96DC3CD584157CA66AAAFDF67E0ED15DD5A0C8A58F3D4BD0F109FC646`. The official consolidated code contains industry- and activity-specific state occupation taxes but no state occupation tax on employment or wage earning. The PDF was used as a working input and not retained because the official HTML provisions and source metadata are sufficient for reproducibility.

Tax Code section 101.008 also bars a city, county, or other political subdivision from levying a Title 2 occupation tax unless state law specifically permits it.

The Texas Municipal League's November 2023 111-page city revenue manual catalogs municipal revenue sources and has no employee wage, earnings, payroll-withholding, or local personal-income-tax chapter. The Comptroller's current city-tax overview likewise identifies property and sales taxes but no employee wage-tax program. These sources corroborate current practice but are not treated as exhaustive negative registries.
