# Municipal Worksite Income Tax Registry and Remaining Closure Plan

## Purpose

This note captures the August 25, 2026 research used to build a national registry of municipal taxes that may require payroll withholding based on where an employee works. It records the agreed definition, current registry status, important exclusions, unresolved cases, and the work needed to close the remaining states.

## Working definition

Include a tax when all of the following are true:

- It is imposed by a municipality or comparable city-level government.
- It applies to employee wages, earnings, compensation, or payroll because the employee works in that jurisdiction.
- It can apply to a nonresident commuter.
- It can create an employer withholding obligation.

The tax may be called an income, earnings, wage, payroll, occupational, or license tax. The name is not decisive; the legal incidence and worksite-based withholding behavior are.

Do not include:

- resident-only taxes;
- employer-only payroll expense taxes;
- flat-dollar occupational or head taxes;
- county, school-district, transit, regional, or other nonmunicipal taxes;
- taxes limited to business profits or self-employment income;
- sales, property, franchise, or business-license taxes; or
- a tax merely because a payroll vendor lists it without authoritative support.

## Current national result

The best-available screening registry contains **2,815 municipality rows across 9 states**:

- **2,636 `CONFIRMED_PRIMARY`** rows supported by a primary government source.
- **179 `SUPPORTED_AUTHORITATIVE_ASSOCIATION`** rows supported by an authoritative municipal association inventory: 20 in Alabama and 159 in Kentucky.

The 2,815 figure is not a claim that every row has been individually verified against a current local ordinance, rate, boundary, and withholding rule. The association-supported rows remain **undetermined for product use** until local primary-source verification is complete.

Of the directly supported rows, 2,619 are in states whose relevant universe has been closed and 17 are known positives in states whose statewide review remains partial.

## State coverage status

### Complete (15)

Alaska, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Kansas, Maryland, Michigan, Missouri, Nebraska, New Jersey, New York, Ohio, Pennsylvania, Vermont, and Wisconsin.

### No qualifying authority confirmed (16)

Arizona, Arkansas, Florida, Georgia, Hawaii, Illinois, Indiana through fiscal year 2027, Louisiana, Minnesota, Nevada, New Mexico, North Carolina, North Dakota, Oklahoma under the worksite definition, Virginia, and Washington.

### Partial (10)

Alabama, Iowa, Kentucky, Maine, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, Oregon, Rhode Island, South Carolina, and Tennessee.

### Undetermined (10)

California, Colorado, Idaho, Mississippi, Montana, South Dakota, Texas, Utah, West Virginia, and Wyoming.

## Important exclusions and boundary decisions

- **New York City:** excluded because its personal income tax is residence-based, not a nonresident worksite wage tax.
- **Baltimore City and other Maryland local income taxes:** excluded because Maryland local income tax follows residence.
- **Portland Arts Tax:** excluded as a flat resident tax.
- **Colorado occupational privilege taxes:** excluded where they are flat-dollar employee charges rather than percentage taxes on wages.
- **Newark and Jersey City employer payroll taxes:** excluded because the employer is the taxpayer.
- **St. Louis payroll-expense tax:** excluded separately from the included employee earnings tax.
- **Ohio JEDD/JEDZ and school-district taxes:** excluded because the registry is municipal-only.
- **Pennsylvania school EIT/PIT and Local Services Tax:** excluded; only the municipal earned-income-tax component is included.
- County, transit, regional, business-profit, net-profit, and self-employment taxes are excluded throughout.

## Open and future candidates

- **Hurstbourne Acres, Kentucky:** secondary payroll sources report a 1% tax effective July 1, 2024, but no current municipal ordinance or form was located. Keep unresolved.
- **Falmouth, Kentucky:** an official page announces a 2026 occupational/payroll ordinance, but the accessible material does not yet prove the complete worksite wage-tax test. Keep unresolved.
- **Salem, Kentucky:** Ordinance 2026-05 appears to qualify but is effective October 1, 2026, so it was future-dated as of the report date.
- **West Virginia:** state law appears to provide conditional authority, but no active adopter has been confirmed.
- **Indiana:** local income-tax authority changes beginning in fiscal year 2028; refresh the registry before that date.

## How to close the undetermined states

Ordinary web searching has reached diminishing returns. The remaining work should be a formal closure audit.

| Priority | States | Closure work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Highest | West Virginia | Inspect Joint Committee records, approval records, pension-plan materials, adopter lists, and termination records to determine whether any conditional municipal wage tax is active. |
| High | Idaho, Mississippi, Montana, South Dakota, Utah, Wyoming | Exhaust current statutes, session laws, special/local acts, and delegations. Seek written confirmation from the legislative research office or responsible tax agency when no complete official list exists. |
| Medium | Colorado | Obtain the official home-rule municipality universe and search every charter and code for percentage-based wage, earnings, payroll, and occupational taxes; distinguish them from flat occupational privilege taxes. |
| Hardest | California, Texas | Review the complete charter/home-rule universe, attorney-general opinions, cases, special laws, and municipal codes. A written state-level confirmation would materially strengthen closure because broad home-rule authority makes keyword-only statutory review insufficient. |

Acceptable closure evidence should be one of the following:

- an explicit current statutory prohibition;
- a complete official authorized-jurisdiction or adopter list;
- an official statement that none exist from the agency responsible for the relevant universe, supported by the legal search; or
- a municipality-by-municipality charter and ordinance review where no statewide source owns the universe.

Payroll-provider databases can identify candidates but should never be treated as final proof.

Online statute, charter, ordinance, case, and agency-record research can continue without external communication. Public-record requests, agency emails, or calls would require separate approval before sending.
