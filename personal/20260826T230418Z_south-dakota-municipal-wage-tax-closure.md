# South Dakota municipal worksite wage-tax closure

Research date: August 26, 2026  
Disposition: `COMPLETE` current zero  
Registry rows added: 0

## Scope

This iteration applied the national registry's narrow definition: a city or city-equivalent percentage tax calculated from an employee's wages, earnings, compensation, or payroll because the employee works there, including nonresident commuters, with employer-withholding relevance.

It excludes resident-only taxes, employer-only payroll expense taxes, flat occupational or head taxes, county or special-district taxes, business profit and gross-receipts taxes, and sales or property taxes.

## Conclusion

No current South Dakota municipality imposes a qualifying employee worksite wage tax. South Dakota moves from `UNDETERMINED` to `COMPLETE` with zero registry rows.

This is a current-adoption conclusion, not a claim that municipal non-ad-valorem authority is categorically impossible. South Dakota Constitution article IX, section 2 gives chartered home-rule units broad powers, and SDCL 10-52-1 and 10-52-2 use broad non-ad-valorem language. `NO_AUTHORITY_CONFIRMED` would therefore overstate the legal result.

## Why the current universe is closed

1. **Central statutory notice and administration.** SDCL 10-52-9 requires a municipality to notify the Secretary of Revenue at least 90 days before any new or amended tax ordinance under chapter 10-52 can take effect. SDCL 10-52-4 makes the Department of Revenue the administrator when the state imposes a similar tax and permits municipal contracts for administration of other chapter 10-52 taxes.
2. **Current authoritative tax inventory.** The Department's July 2026 *Municipal Tax Guide* states that SDCL chapters 10-52 and 10-52A allow municipalities to impose municipal sales/use and gross-receipts taxes, identifies DOR as their administrative and enforcement agency, and supplies the current municipality-by-municipality tax-code inventory. It identifies no employee income, wage, earnings, compensation, or payroll tax category or adopter.
3. **Current code audit.** The audit downloaded all 63 official consolidated title files—South Dakota Constitution title `0N` plus codified-law titles 1 through 62—and searched the complete 176,375,083-byte corpus. It found no exact `wage tax`, `earnings tax`, `compensation tax`, `municipal income tax`, or `city income tax` phrase. The single `payroll tax` occurrence is a sales-tax deduction for professional-employer-organization disbursements in SDCL 10-45-96. The two `local income tax` occurrences are generic partnership recordkeeping provisions in title 48, not South Dakota municipal taxing authority.
4. **No state individual-income-tax system to mirror.** SDCL 10-64-1 expressly says South Dakota has no income tax. The separate financial-institution income tax in chapter 10-43 is state-administered and section 10-43-5 makes it in lieu of nearly all local taxes on those institutions; it does not create a municipal employee wage tax.
5. **Additional local-power guardrail.** SDCL 6-12-14 applies to every city, including home-rule cities, and generally bars a local tax similar to one producing state revenue unless statute specifically provides otherwise. The closure does not depend on treating this provision as a categorical individual-income-tax prohibition; the current DOR inventory and complete code search close the adopter universe directly.

## Limitations

The DOR materials describe the current centrally recognized municipal-tax system, not every fee a municipality might label as a charge. The complete-code audit resolves the statutory universe, while the DOR guide and chapter 10-52 notice topology resolve current adoption for the included tax. The conclusion must be refreshed when one of the stated triggers occurs.
