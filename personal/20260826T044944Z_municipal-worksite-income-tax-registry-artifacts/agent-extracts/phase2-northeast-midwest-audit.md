# Narrow worksite municipal employee-wage tax audit

Audit date: 2026-08-25

Scope: Connecticut, Iowa, Kansas, Maine, Massachusetts, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, Rhode Island, Vermont, West Virginia, and Wisconsin.

## Inclusion rule

A row qualifies only when a **municipality** imposes a tax whose legal incidence is on an employee's wages or earned income because the employee works in the municipality, including nonresidents, and the employer must withhold it. Employer-incidence payroll taxes, resident-only income surtaxes, flat service/user fees, county/school/regional taxes, unemployment or pension contributions, and taxes on businesses or self-employment are excluded.

`COMPLETE` means the current primary-source topology closes the statewide municipal universe for this narrow question. `PARTIAL` means current sources support no positive row, but a special-act/charter or other residual authority universe was not conclusively closed. `UNDETERMINED` means a current statute creates facially qualifying authority but an active adopter could not be confirmed or disproved from an official statewide adopter source.

## State evidence

### Connecticut

**Conclusion:** zero qualifying municipalities; `COMPLETE`.

- Current municipal powers are codified in [Conn. Gen. Stat. Chapter 98, Municipal Powers](https://www.cga.ct.gov/current/pub/chap_098.htm). Section 7-148(c)(2)(B) permits municipalities to tax only property, subjects, or objects that may lawfully be taxed; it is not an independent municipal income-tax grant.
- The Connecticut General Assembly Office of Legislative Research explains that Connecticut municipalities have only powers conferred by statute or special act and that **municipalities can levy only property taxes**: [OLR Report 2014-R-0037, Property Tax and Local Government Finance](https://www.cga.ct.gov/2014/rpt/2014-R-0037.htm). This statewide statement resolves the potential special-act concern for the present audit.
- Current 2026 employee withholding is expressly Connecticut State income tax, including nonresident wages for services in Connecticut: [Connecticut DRS, 2026 Withholding Tax Information](https://portal.ct.gov/drs/withholding-taxes/tax-information) (current 2026 page; nonresident rules at the same URL).

**Excluded ambiguity:** State income tax on nonresident worksite wages. It satisfies a worksite rule but is not municipal.

### Iowa

**Conclusion:** no qualifying municipality found; `PARTIAL`.

- Iowa's current city-finance universe is [Iowa Code 2026, Chapter 384, City Finance](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2026/384.pdf). Its city levies are stated as rates per assessed property value, including the general fund and additional levies.
- Iowa's broad city home rule is subject to inconsistency with General Assembly law: [Iowa Code 2026, Chapter 364, §§ 364.1-.3](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2026/364.pdf). The code does not contain a Kansas/Wisconsin-style express city income-tax prohibition in the reviewed provisions.
- Iowa does authorize a **county** local income surtax for emergency medical services, but it is imposed on the State individual income tax of an individual **residing in the county at year end**, not on nonresident worksite wages: [Iowa Code 2026, Chapter 422D, §§ 422D.1-.4](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2026/422D.pdf). It is excluded both as county-level and resident-only.
- The 2026 Code's official city-finance chapter and current State income-tax framework disclosed no employer-withheld municipal employee wage tax. However, the official materials reviewed do not conclusively eliminate every special act or charter permitted by home rule.

**Precise gap:** no official statewide municipal tax-type register or explicit preemption provision was found that closes every special-act/charter possibility.

### Kansas

**Conclusion:** zero qualifying municipalities; `COMPLETE`.

- [2025 K.S.A. 12-140, Cities prohibited from levying taxes on income; exception](https://www.kslegislature.gov/media/statute/012_000_0000_chapter/012_001_0000_article/012_001_0040_section/012_001_0040_k.pdf) provides: except as specifically authorized by K.S.A. 12-1,101 through 12-1,109, no city has power to levy and collect taxes on income from any source.
- The exception is the local-option tax on gross earnings from money, notes, and other evidence of debt, catalogued by the Legislature in [Kansas Statutes, Chapter 12, Article 1](https://www.kslegislature.gov/li_2024s/b2023_24/statute/012_000_0000_chapter/012_001_0000_article/). It is investment/intangibles income, not employee wages and not employer worksite withholding.

**Excluded ambiguity:** local-option intangibles tax; it does not tax wages for services.

### Maine

**Conclusion:** no qualifying municipality found; `PARTIAL`.

- Maine's municipal ordinance power is broad and creates a rebuttable presumption of validity unless authority is denied or State law would be frustrated: [30-A M.R.S. § 3001, Ordinance power](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/30-A/title30-Asec3001.html) (official page extracted 2025-10-23).
- Current municipal taxation materials are property-tax based. [36 M.R.S. § 503, Town taxes; legality](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/36/title36sec503.html) sits in Part 2, Property Taxes, Chapter 105, Cities and Towns. Maine Revenue Services' current municipal training is likewise [PT101, Introduction to Property Tax Assessment](https://www.maine.gov/future/sites/maine.gov.revenue/files/inline-files/pt101_text.pdf) (2026 edition).
- Maine individual income tax and employer withholding are State programs administered by Maine Revenue Services: [Individual Income Tax (1040ME)](https://www1.maine.gov/revenue/taxes/income-estate-tax/individual-income-tax-1040me) and [MRS Income/Estate Tax Division](https://www.maine.gov/REVENUE/incomeestate/homepage.html).

**Precise gap:** Maine's home-rule presumption means absence from the general municipal-finance and State-tax sources is not alone a closed proof against every special charter/act or locally asserted authority.

### Massachusetts

**Conclusion:** no qualifying municipality found; `PARTIAL`.

- The Massachusetts Constitution's Home Rule Amendment, Article 89 § 7, says the amendment does **not** grant a city or town power to levy, assess, and collect taxes, while allowing the General Court to grant such power: [Massachusetts Constitution, Article 89, §§ 6-8](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/Constitution).
- Article XLIV grants the General Court power to impose an income tax and requires uniformity throughout the Commonwealth for income of the same class: [Massachusetts Constitution, Article XLIV](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/Constitution).
- No current general law or official municipal form was found imposing an employee-incidence municipal wage tax with nonresident worksite withholding.

**Precise gap:** Article 89 preserves municipality-specific special laws. This audit did not exhaust every enacted home-rule petition/special act, so it does not claim a fully closed special-law universe.

### Nebraska

**Conclusion:** zero qualifying municipalities; `COMPLETE`.

- The Nebraska Legislature's official fiscal-policy resource states: **"Nebraska has never authorized local income taxes"**: [Nebraska Legislature, Taxes and Spending in Nebraska — Individual Income Tax](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/app_rev/source/indlincometax.htm).
- Current individual income tax is imposed by the State on residents and Nebraska-source income of nonresidents: [Neb. Rev. Stat. § 77-2715](https://www.nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=77-2715).
- Current city-class taxing authority is property based; for example, metropolitan-class cities annually certify tax on taxable property: [Neb. Rev. Stat. § 14-514](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=14-514).

**Excluded ambiguity:** municipal occupation taxes and State income withholding. No local employee-incidence wage tax is authorized.

### New Hampshire

**Conclusion:** no qualifying municipality found; `PARTIAL`.

- The New Hampshire Department of Revenue Administration states the State has no income tax on reported W-2 wages, and the former Interest and Dividends Tax is repealed for taxable periods beginning after 2024-12-31: [NHDRA, Interest & Dividends Tax](https://www.revenue.nh.gov/taxes-glance/interest-dividends-tax).
- NHDRA's current tax universe lists business, consumption, property, transfer, and other State taxes but no municipal wage tax: [NHDRA, Taxes at a Glance](https://www.revenue.nh.gov/taxes-glance).
- The Business Enterprise Tax can use compensation in its base, but it is a State tax imposed on the business enterprise, not an employee-incidence municipal tax: [NHDRA, 2024 BET Quick Checklist](https://www.revenue.nh.gov/sites/g/files/ehbemt736/files/documents/bet-checklist-2024.pdf).

**Precise gap:** State sources strongly support zero and no W-2 tax, but the reviewed primary sources did not expressly preempt all municipality-specific charter/special-act taxing authority.

### New Jersey

**Conclusion:** zero qualifying municipalities; `COMPLETE` for the narrow employee-incidence definition.

- New Jersey employee income-tax withholding is on behalf of the **State**, including nonresidents working in New Jersey: [NJ Division of Taxation, Employer Payroll Tax](https://www.nj.gov/treasury/taxation/businesses/payroll/) (last updated 2025-04-30).
- Newark imposes a 1% tax on employers' payroll measured by wages for services performed or supervised from Newark: [City of Newark, Payroll Tax](https://www.newarknj.gov/647/Payroll-Tax). The City's business-registration page identifies the legal authority as N.J.S.A. 40:48C-14 et seq. and calls it an **Employer Payroll Tax**: [City of Newark, Payroll-tax licensing requirement](https://www.newarknj.gov/331/Ice).
- Jersey City imposes a 1% tax on employers' gross payroll, excludes Jersey City residents' wages from the employer's base, and expressly says employers **may not deduct or withhold** the tax from employees: [City of Jersey City, Payroll Tax FAQ](https://www.jerseycitynj.gov/cityhall/finance/taxes/payrolltaxfaq). The operative official ordinance is [Jersey City Ordinance 18-133](https://www.jerseycitynj.gov/userfiles/servers/server_6189660/file/city%20hall/tax%20collections/payroll%20tax%20faq/ord%2018-133%20payroll%20tax.pdf), Article IV, particularly §§ 304-18 through 304-21.

**Incidence ruling:** Newark and Jersey City are employer-liability payroll taxes whose measure references employee wages. They are not employee wage taxes remitted by withholding and produce no registry rows.

### Rhode Island

**Conclusion:** no qualifying municipality found; `PARTIAL`.

- The current general city/town levy authority permits tax on the **ratable property** of the city or town: [R.I. Gen. Laws § 44-5-1, Powers of city or town electors to levy](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/statutes/title44/44-5/44-5-1.HTM).
- Rhode Island personal income tax is State-administered and covers residents and Rhode Island-source income of nonresidents: [RI Division of Taxation, Personal Income Tax](https://tax.ri.gov/tax-sections/personal-income-tax).
- Current resident and nonresident returns, including State withholding schedules, are in the State form set: [RI Division of Taxation, 2025 Personal Income Tax Forms](https://tax.ri.gov/forms/individual-tax-forms/personal-income-tax-forms).
- The State's Division of Municipal Finance identifies municipal tax-levy data as property-tax/revaluation finance, with no municipal wage withholding system: [RI Division of Municipal Finance](https://municipalfinance.ri.gov/).

**Excluded ambiguity:** Rhode Island-source nonresident income is State, not city/town, tax.

**Precise gap:** the Rhode Island Constitution reserves municipal taxing authority to the General Assembly, which can enact municipality-specific tax-enabling laws. The current general-law and official municipal-finance topology supports zero, but this audit did not exhaust every special act.

### Vermont

**Conclusion:** zero qualifying municipalities; `COMPLETE`.

- Current 24 V.S.A. § 138 authorizes municipalities to impose only a 1% sales tax, 1% meals and alcoholic beverages tax, and 1% rooms tax; "municipality" means city, town, or incorporated village: [24 V.S.A. § 138, Local option taxes](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/24/005/00138) (statutes include 2025 session actions; October 1, 2025 amendments shown).
- The official municipal-charter corpus is maintained as Title 24 Appendix. Targeted searches for `income tax`, `payroll tax`, and `occupational tax` found no municipal employee-wage grant; located charter tax provisions were property and the § 138-style local-option taxes: [Vermont Legislature, Municipal Charters](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/title/24APPENDIX).
- Vermont's Child Care Contribution is a **State** payroll contribution administered by the Department of Taxes. Employers owe 0.44% of covered wages and may withhold no more than one quarter from employees: [32 V.S.A. §§ 10552-10553](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/32/246/10553) (effective 2024-07-01). It is excluded because it is statewide, not municipal.

**Excluded ambiguity:** statewide Child Care Contribution; municipal sales/meals/rooms local options.

### West Virginia

**Conclusion:** no active qualifying municipality confirmed; `UNDETERMINED`.

#### Facially qualifying statutory path

- A "qualifying municipality" must have a weighted police/fire pension funding percentage of 3% or less at ordinance adoption and must satisfy § 8-13C-11: [W. Va. Code § 8-13C-2](https://code.wvlegislature.gov/8-13C-2/).
- It may impose a uniform pension-relief municipal occupational tax of 1% or less on salaries, wages, commissions, and other earned income of taxable employees, and each employer must withhold and remit the tax each pay period: [W. Va. Code § 8-13C-3](https://code.wvlegislature.gov/8-13C-3/).
- A taxable employee is an individual employed by an employer with a place of business in the municipality and earning more than $10,000 annually: [W. Va. Code § 8-13C-2](https://code.wvlegislature.gov/8-13C-2/). On its face, this is worksite/employer-location incidence and is not restricted to municipal residents.
- Authority is **not effective** until the municipality presents a pension-liability plan to the Joint Committee on Government and Finance and necessary changes in West Virginia law are enacted: [W. Va. Code § 8-13C-11](https://code.wvlegislature.gov/8-13C-11/).
- Authority terminates on the statutory pension-funding/bond conditions: [W. Va. Code § 8-13C-9](https://code.wvlegislature.gov/8-13C-9/).

#### Excluded municipal charges confirmed current

- General municipal service-charge authority permits reasonable rates, fees, and charges on users of police, fire, street, sanitation, and other services: [W. Va. Code § 8-13-13](https://code.wvlegislature.gov/8-13-13/). These are service charges, not percentage wage taxes.
- Huntington currently charges $5 per week to individuals working in city limits, withheld by employers: [City of Huntington, City Service Fee](https://www.cityofhuntington.com/business/taxes-and-fees/city-service-fee/) and [official current return instructions](https://www.cityofhuntington.com/assets/pdf/document-center/City_Service_Fee_%28CSF%29_Return_City_of_Huntington_Fillable.pdf). Flat weekly fee: excluded.
- Charleston currently charges $3 per week to full-time/part-time employees and self-employed individuals working in the City, withheld by employers: [City of Charleston, Start a Business — City Service Fee](https://www.charlestonwv.gov/node/143) and [City Service Fee FAQ](https://www.charlestonwv.gov/sites/default/files/non-departmental-documents/2022-07/City%20Service%20Fee%20FAQ.pdf). Flat weekly fee: excluded.
- Wheeling's official ordinance and regulations impose $2 per calendar week and require employer withholding: [Wheeling Article 797 ordinance](https://www.wheelingwv.gov/media/City%20Clerk/Agenda%20Links/2019/August%206%202019/City%20Service%20Fee-%20Final.pdf) and [Wheeling City Service Fee regulations](https://www.wheelingwv.gov/media/Finance/City%20Service%20Fee/City%20Service%20Fee%20-%20Regulations.pdf). Flat weekly fee: excluded.
- Municipal B&O/privilege taxes are taxes on persons engaging in business and are measured by gross receipts/value, not employee-incidence wages: [W. Va. Code § 8-13-5](https://code.wvlegislature.gov/8-13-5/).
- Municipal home-rule plans may not enact an occupation tax, fee, or assessment payable by a nonresident; this blocks use of home rule to create a separate nonresident worksite wage tax outside Article 13C: [W. Va. Code § 8-1-5a](https://code.wvlegislature.gov/8-1-5a/).

#### Precise unresolved gap

No current official statewide register was found identifying municipalities that (1) satisfied § 8-13C-11, (2) remained at or below the qualifying threshold when adopting an ordinance, (3) enacted a percentage occupational-tax ordinance, and (4) retain authority under § 8-13C-9. Targeted searches of the Legislature, Municipal Pensions Oversight Board, State Auditor/local-government materials, and current municipal tax/form pages found the statute and flat service fees, but no current percentage-tax return or active municipal ordinance. Therefore the statute cannot be converted into a municipality row, and absence cannot be upgraded to a closed zero.

**Phase-2 follow-up needed:** obtain a written current adopter/non-adopter confirmation from the West Virginia Municipal Pensions Oversight Board or Joint Committee on Government and Finance, or locate the municipal plan, enabling act, ordinance, current rate, and employer return for each adopter.

### Wisconsin

**Conclusion:** zero qualifying municipalities; `COMPLETE`.

- [Wis. Stat. § 66.0611, Political subdivisions prohibited from levying tax on incomes](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/66/vi/0611) provides that no county, city, village, town, or other taxing unit may assess, levy, or collect a tax on income or measured by income, and any such tax is void. The current 2025 text is also available in the Legislature's [Chapter 66 PDF](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/66.pdf).
- Current Wisconsin employer withholding tools are State income-tax tools: [Wisconsin DOR Withholding Calculator](https://www.revenue.wi.gov/Pages/Apps/Withholding-Calculator.aspx).

**Excluded ambiguity:** State individual income tax is described in Wis. Stat. § 71.02 as raising revenue for the State and local governments, but it remains a single State-imposed/administered tax; § 66.0611 bars a separate political-subdivision income tax.

## Reproducible registry decision notes

1. Search labels are not incidence. A municipal item called "payroll tax" is excluded when the employer is the taxpayer and employee withholding is prohibited (NJ).
2. A worksite-triggered amount is not enough. Flat weekly employee service/user fees are excluded even when employers withhold them (WV).
3. A resident local income surtax is excluded when it does not reach nonresident worksite wages and/or is not municipal (IA county EMS surtax).
4. A State payroll contribution or State nonresident income tax is excluded even when an employer withholds it (CT, NJ, RI, VT).
5. Statutory authority without a confirmed effective adopter is not a registry row (WV Article 13C).
