# Southern-state municipal worksite wage-tax audit

**Scope.** Alabama, Georgia, Kentucky, Louisiana, Mississippi, South Carolina, and Tennessee. Retrieval/revalidation date: **2026-08-25**. A row is in scope only when a municipality (or consolidated municipal equivalent) imposes a percentage tax on an employee's wages/compensation for work performed there, including commuters, and the employer must or may be required to withhold. Resident-only, employer-only, flat-fee, county/school/region, business-profit, and self-employment-only taxes are excluded.

**Evidence rule.** Only primary public sources are used below: current state statutes/constitutional text or official enactment material, state revenue/commerce administrators, and municipal codes/forms/pages. `COMPLETE` means the official source closes the current statewide universe. `PARTIAL` means one or more positives are directly proved but the adopter set is not closed. `UNDETERMINED` means no positive was proved and the available primary sources do not close all possible authority.

## State source/evidence notes

### Alabama

- [Act 2020-14 / HB 147 official bill text](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/files/pdf/SearchableInstruments/2020RS/PrintFiles/204606-1.pdf) provides that the Legislature may authorize a municipality by local law to impose a tax on a natural person derived from a vocation/occupation within the municipality and preserves municipal occupational taxes in effect before 2020-02-01. Effective 2020-03-03 per the [ALDOR 2020 general summary](https://www.revenue.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/2020-General-Summary.pdf).
- [ALDOR 2024 withholding booklet](https://www.revenue.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/whbooklet_1024.pdf) says local occupational taxes are not administered by ALDOR and directs taxpayers to the administering city or county. Therefore it cannot close the municipal adopter universe.
- **Unresolved current candidate — Bessemer.** The [current city forms page](https://bessemeral.org/business/business-licenses-forms) posts quarterly and annual occupational-tax forms, and the city's [FY2023 audited financial statements](https://www.bessemeral.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/City-of-Bessemer-September-30-2023-Audited-Financial-Statement.pdf) report occupational-tax revenue. The accessible current source set does not state the worksite/nonresident rule. An older budget calls it a “one cent” tax, but that is insufficient for a strict current registry row. Exact blocker: current ordinance/form instructions establishing employee incidence and inside/outside allocation.
- Excluded: county occupational taxes (for example Macon County statutory authority) are nonmunicipal.

### Georgia

- [Georgia General Assembly 2010 General Statutes Summary](https://www.legis.ga.gov/api/document/docs/default-source/general-statutes/2010sumdoc.pdf) states that Act 382/HB 984 enacted O.C.G.A. §48-7-140 to prohibit levy or collection of local income taxes, effective 2010-05-20.
- [Georgia DOR 2010 enacted-legislation analysis](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/legislative-summary/2010-legislation-summary/download) states HB 984 repealed the article that had given municipalities authority to adopt a local income tax, effective 2010-05-20. The enacted statutory command is categorical: no local income tax may be levied or collected by any political subdivision and no local return may be required.
- Business occupational taxes measured by a business's gross receipts (for example municipal occupation-tax certificates under Title 48, chapter 13) are excluded; they are not taxes on employee wages.

### Kentucky

- [Kentucky Secretary of State occupational-tax portal](https://web.sos.ky.gov/occupationaltax/) says it provides forms for Kentucky taxing districts pursuant to KRS 67.766, but expressly warns: “There may be omissions or inaccuracies” and directs users to local districts for current information. It also mixes counties and cities and does not classify wage versus net-profit taxes. It is authoritative discovery material but not a complete registry.
- [Kentucky DOR business-tax page](https://revenue.ky.gov/Business/Pages/default.aspx) says DOR does not administer local occupational, net-profits, or gross-receipts taxes and directs users to local officials.
- **Provisional current candidate — West Buechel.** The [current city forms page](https://westbuechel.ky.gov/payment-and-forms/Pages/Forms.aspx) states “West Buechel Local City Tax 1.5% of wages (Quarterly),” and the city posts [Ordinance 315 Series 2025 amending occupational-tax chapter 113](https://westbuechel.ky.gov/payment-and-forms/PublishingImages/Pages/Forms/Ord%20315%20Series%202025%20AMENDING%20VARIOUS%20SECTIONS%20OF%20CHAPTER%20113%20-%20OCCUPATIONAL%20LICENSE%20TAX%20OF%20THE%20CITY%20OF%20WEST%20BUECHEL%20CODE%20OF%20ORDINANCES.pdf). The ordinance PDF was not text-extractable in the research interface, and the forms page alone does not expressly state commuter/worksite incidence. Keep as review-only until the current ordinance is manually verified for nonresident/worksite scope and employer withholding.
- Counties, school-board components, business net-profit taxes, and 1099/self-employment taxes are excluded. Louisville's resident-only 0.75% school-board increment is not part of the commuter registry rate.

### Louisiana

- [Louisiana Constitution art. VII, §4](https://www.legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=206559), current Legislature text, states at §4(C): “A political subdivision of the state shall not levy a ... income tax.”
- [Louisiana Constitution art. VI, §44 definitions](https://senate.legis.la.gov/Documents/Constitution/Article6.htm) defines political subdivision to include parish, municipality, and other local-government units, and municipality as an incorporated city, town, or village.
- The prohibition is direct and contains no current-municipal grandfather relevant to this scope. Candidate count: zero.

### Mississippi

- Current Miss. Code §21-17-5(2), available through the [Legislature's Mississippi Code portal](https://www.legislature.ms.gov/general-information/), provides that municipal home-rule power does not authorize levying taxes of any kind unless specifically authorized elsewhere by statute or law. This forecloses inference from general home rule, but it does not itself enumerate every special/local authorization.
- [Mississippi DOR tax structure](https://www.dor.ms.gov/forms-resources/taxpayer-guidance/mississippi-tax-structure) lists the state-administered income/withholding system but does not claim a complete local-tax universe.
- **Excluded look-alike:** [Mississippi DOR individual-income-tax instructions](https://www.dor.ms.gov/sites/default/files/Forms/indiv_80100138.pdf) describe a Job Development Assessment Fee withheld from employee wages and shown in the W-2 city/local box, but say the assessment services **Business Finance Corporation-issued bonds**. This is not evidence of a municipality-imposed tax and is excluded as nonmunicipal absent a municipal levying source.
- Exact blocker for a definitive zero: an official, current closed list of all statutes/local acts specifically authorizing municipal wage/compensation taxes, or a categorical statutory prohibition.

### South Carolina

- [Current South Carolina Department of Commerce Incentives & Taxes page](https://www.sccommerce.com/why-sc/incentives-taxes) states “No local income tax.”
- [S.C. Code §6-1-310](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t06c001.php) provides: “A local governing body may not impose a new tax after December 31, 1996, unless specifically authorized by the General Assembly.”
- [Current SCDOR withholding page](https://dor.sc.gov/withholding) describes withholding only for South Carolina income-tax liability and remittance to SCDOR; it identifies no municipal wage withholding.
- Exact blocker to `COMPLETE`: §6-1-310 has a pre-1997 temporal boundary and allows specific authorization; the Commerce statement uses the label “income tax” and is not itself a closed code inventory for differently labeled compensation-based occupational taxes.

### Tennessee

- [Official HJR 35 amendment text](https://www.capitol.tn.gov/Bills/108/Bill/HJR0035.pdf), adopted by voters in 2014, added to Tenn. Const. art. II, §28: the Legislature may not levy, authorize, or permit any state or local tax on or measured by payroll or earned personal income. The same sentence preserves any tax in effect on 2011-01-01.
- The current General Assembly reaffirmed the operative amendment in [HJR 17 (114th General Assembly)](https://capitol.tn.gov/Bills/114/Bill/HJR0017.pdf), stating that Amendment 3 was approved in 2014 and prohibits state or local payroll/earned-income taxes.
- [Tennessee DOR Hall Income Tax Manual](https://comptroller.extglb.tn.gov/content/dam/tn/revenue/documents/tax_manuals/august-2022/Hall-Income-Tax.pdf) states the repealed Hall tax did not apply to salary, wages, or commissions and was fully repealed for tax years beginning on/after 2021-01-01. It is not a qualifying wage tax.
- Flat municipal privilege/occupation licenses are excluded. Exact blocker to `COMPLETE`: no current official statewide source was found enumerating all taxes preserved by the 2011 grandfather clause and proving none is a municipal employee worksite wage tax.

## Bottom line

- **Direct closed statutory zeros:** Georgia and Louisiana.
- **Direct current positive rows:** 14 (Alabama 5, Kentucky 9).
- **No positive but not fully closed:** Mississippi, South Carolina, Tennessee, for the precise source gaps stated above.
- **Do not auto-add:** Bessemer or West Buechel until the missing current worksite/nonresident incidence text is verified from their current ordinance/form instructions.
