## Method and classification

The legal topology closes both general-law and home-rule municipalities. Article VIII, section 1(f) caps a municipal occupation tax at half the state tax on the same pursuit. *Brown* and Attorney General Opinion JM-1195 apply that formula as a prohibition when the state levies no occupation tax on the pursuit. *Conlen Grain* classifies a revenue exaction by its subject—the privilege of carrying on the occupation—not merely by its calculation formula. A tax imposed on an employee for working in a city is consequently an occupation tax even if measured by wages.

Texas has no state occupation tax on employment or wage earning. Article XI, section 5 and Tax Code chapter 302 do not allow a charter or ordinance to exceed the constitutional ceiling. No city-by-city charter audit is required because the statewide constitutional rule closes the authority path.

The Texas Municipal League revenue manual and Comptroller city-tax materials were used as current-practice corroboration, not as the dispositive negative proof.
