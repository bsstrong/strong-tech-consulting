# Utah municipal worksite wage-tax closure

Research date: August 26, 2026  
Disposition: `NO_AUTHORITY_CONFIRMED`  
Registry rows added: 0

## Scope

This iteration applied the national registry's narrow definition: a city or city-equivalent percentage tax calculated from an employee's wages, earnings, compensation, or payroll because the employee works there, including nonresident commuters, with employer-withholding relevance.

It excludes resident-only taxes, employer-only payroll expense taxes, flat occupational or head taxes, county or special-district taxes, business profit and gross-receipts taxes, and sales or property taxes.

## Conclusion

Utah municipalities have no current authority to impose a qualifying employee worksite wage tax. Utah moves from `UNDETERMINED` to `NO_AUTHORITY_CONFIRMED` with zero registry rows.

The conclusion rests on a controlling rule of municipal tax power, the current constitutional limit, and a complete audit of the current Utah Code—not merely on the absence of a city from a tax-agency page.

## Why the legal universe is closed

1. **Municipal tax power requires a state-law grant.** Utah Constitution article XI, section 5 limits a charter city's taxing power to the bounds prescribed by general law. The Utah Supreme Court held in *Moss v. Board of Commissioners of Salt Lake City*, 1 Utah 2d 60, 261 P.2d 961 (1953), that a city's power to tax comes solely from legislative enactment and includes only authority expressly conferred or necessarily implied; ambiguity is resolved for the taxpayer.
2. **The general business-license provision does not reach employees.** Current Utah Code section 10-1-203 expressly excludes employees rendering services to employers from its definition of a taxable or regulable `Business`. The section then enumerates municipal revenue authority for energy sales/use, telecommunications, parking services, public-assembly tickets, and certain high-cost or enhanced-service businesses. It does not authorize a tax on employee wages, earnings, compensation, or payroll.
3. **Complete current-code audit.** The official current-code index identified 96 current title PDFs. All 96 were downloaded and extracted: 19,339 pages, 37,553,810 PDF bytes, and 56,391,062 extracted characters. The corpus contains no exact `wage tax`, `earnings tax`, `compensation tax`, `municipal income tax`, `city income tax`, or `political subdivision income tax` phrase. Every nonzero `payroll tax`, `local income tax`, and municipal/compensation proximity hit was manually inspected and rejected as an unrelated federal/state withholding, recordkeeping, bond, deduction, or FICA provision.
4. **The actual withholding system is state income tax.** Current section 59-10-402 requires an employer paying wages to withhold the income tax imposed by Utah's state individual-income-tax chapter. The April 2026 Utah Withholding Tax Guide likewise describes one Utah withholding account and Utah tax calculated from employee wages; neither source delegates or administers a municipal wage tax.

This is a current-law authority conclusion, not a prediction that Utah will never authorize a local wage tax. The complete-code selection used the current effective version displayed for each title in the official index and excluded separately displayed future-effective duplicates. The conclusion should be refreshed on the stated legal triggers.
