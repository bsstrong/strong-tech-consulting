# Mississippi municipal worksite wage-tax closure

## Outcome

**Disposition: `NO_AUTHORITY_CONFIRMED`; zero registry rows.**

As of August 26, 2026, no Mississippi municipality has current legal authority or a current levy matching this registry's percentage-based, employer-withheld employee worksite wage-tax definition. Mississippi therefore moves from `UNDETERMINED` to a closed zero. The national registry remains 2,815 municipality rows across nine states.

This iteration took approximately 20 minutes, within the one-hour state cap.

## Definition applied

The registry includes only a municipality or city-like government's percentage tax calculated from an employee's wages, earnings, compensation, or payroll because the employee works there, including a nonresident commuter, with employer-withholding relevance.

It excludes resident-only income taxes, employer-only payroll-expense taxes, flat occupational or head taxes, sales/property/business taxes, county or district taxes, and taxes limited to business profits or self-employment.

## Why Mississippi is closed

1. **A municipality needs a specific tax grant.** [Mississippi Code section 21-17-5(2)](https://law.justia.com/codes/mississippi/title-21/chapter-17/section-21-17-5/) expressly says municipal home rule does not authorize a municipality to levy any tax or increase an authorized tax unless another state statute or law specifically authorizes the action. The [Secretary of State](https://www.sos.ms.gov/publications-external-affairs/mississippi-law) identifies LexisNexis as the official publisher of the searchable unannotated code; the current 2025 text was cross-checked through the accessible code publication.

2. **The current municipal-tax framework contains no wage-tax grant.** Current Title 21, chapter 33 enumerates municipal taxation and finance, including ad valorem and city utility taxes, but contains no authority for a percentage municipal tax on an employee's wages, earnings, compensation, or payroll. The Mississippi Office of the State Auditor's mandatory [Municipal Audit and Accounting Guide](https://www.osa.ms.gov/sites/default/files/Resources/Local%20Governments/maag22.pdf) likewise identifies municipal tax receipts and compliance procedures for ad valorem, local privilege, vending-machine, and transient-vendor taxes. Its prescribed municipal chart of accounts and audit procedures contain no employee income, wage, earnings, or payroll tax.

3. **The current state-administered local-levy universe contains no qualifying tax.** The Department of Revenue's current [withholding-tax program](https://www.dor.ms.gov/business/withholding-tax) is Mississippi income-tax withholding. Its current city and special-levy material covers sales, tourism, hotel, restaurant, occupancy, utility, and similar taxes. The Department's January 2026 statewide special-levy distribution report lists the active special local levies and contains no employee income, wage, earnings, compensation, or payroll levy.

4. **The Legislature repeatedly considered—but did not enact—the exact missing authority.** I retrieved the official “All Measures” report for every regular session from 1997 through 2026 and searched all 30 reports. The only bill titles containing “local income tax” were 1997 HB 68, 1998 HB 299, 1999 HB 950, 2001 HB 900, and 2003 HB 1467. Every bill died in House committee. The introduced bills would have specifically authorized eligible cities to tax state taxable income from employment or business carried on in the city, required nonresident work allocation, and used state tax administration. Their repeated failure is consistent with section 21-17-5's specific-authorization rule and leaves no enacted general municipal income-tax grant.

5. **The wage-deducted Job Development Assessment Fee is not a municipal tax.** Department of Revenue instructions describe a project-specific assessment on employees whose jobs were created through Mississippi Business Finance Corporation bonds and direct it to the “city or local” W-2 box using `RED`. The assessment services corporation-issued project bonds; it is not imposed by a municipality and is outside the registry regardless of whether a legacy project remains active.

The conclusion does not rest on a general web-search absence. It combines the specific-authorization rule, the current codified municipal-tax framework, the State Auditor's mandatory all-municipality accounting/compliance framework, the Department of Revenue's current withholding and special-levy systems, and a 30-regular-session legislative audit.

## Caveat and refresh rule

`NO_AUTHORITY_CONFIRMED` is a current legal conclusion, not a promise that Mississippi law cannot change. Refresh after any amendment to section 21-17-5, Title 21 chapter 33, a new general or local/private act authorizing a municipal tax measured by employee compensation, or Department of Revenue/State Auditor material disclosing a new municipal employee withholding or wage-tax account.
