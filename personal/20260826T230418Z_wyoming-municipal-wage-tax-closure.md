# Wyoming municipal worksite wage-tax closure

Research date: August 26, 2026  
Disposition: `NO_AUTHORITY_CONFIRMED`  
Registry rows added: 0

## Scope

This iteration applied the national registry's narrow definition: a city or city-equivalent percentage tax calculated from an employee's wages, earnings, compensation, or payroll because the employee works there, including nonresident commuters, with employer-withholding relevance.

It excludes resident-only taxes, employer-only payroll expense taxes, flat occupational or head taxes, county or special-district taxes, business profit and gross-receipts taxes, and sales or property taxes.

## Conclusion

Wyoming law expressly prohibits every county, city, town, and other political subdivision from imposing, levying, or collecting an income tax, earnings tax, or any other tax based on wages or other income. Wyoming moves from `UNDETERMINED` to `NO_AUTHORITY_CONFIRMED` with zero registry rows.

## Controlling authority

Current Wyoming Statutes section 39-12-101 states that Wyoming preempts the field of income taxes, earnings taxes, and every other tax based on wages or other income. It expressly denies counties, cities, towns, and other political subdivisions the right to impose, levy, or collect those taxes.

The current constitutional structure independently requires the same statutory foundation: article 13, section 3 says municipal taxes and assessments may be levied or collected only pursuant to law for public purposes specified by law, and article 15, section 13 says no tax may be levied except pursuant to law.
